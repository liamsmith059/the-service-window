About the Sommelier

Wilhelm Smithers

Chef sommelier

Wilhelm Smithers came to wine the way some men come to religion: late, quietly, and because of Joel Friesen. Little is known of Friesen. He is said to have cooked for three summers in a kitchen with no name, somewhere between Lyon and the sea, and to have left each one without notice. No photograph of him is confirmed. What survives are the recipes, copied by hand and passed between cooks who do not discuss where they got them, and a handful of pairings so exact that cellar masters still argue over how he arrived at them. Smithers encountered one of those pairings at nineteen. He has spent every year since trying to understand it. He does not say which one it was.

The Service Window exists to give every meal the attention usually reserved for a tasting menu. The wines are real, the regions are real, and the reasoning is the same reasoning a sommelier would use on any plate: fat, salt, sugar, acid, heat. Only the plates have changed. Order what you like. We will tell you what to pour.
